# Hatch-Waxman and BPCIA exclusivity mechanics

## Small molecules (Hatch-Waxman, 1984)

**Regulatory exclusivities** (independent of patents):

- **NCE** — 5 years from approval of a new chemical entity. An ANDA with a Paragraph IV
  certification may be filed after 4 years.
- **NCI/NPP ("new clinical investigation")** — 3 years for a supplement supported by new
  clinical work; protects the change, not the molecule.
- **ODE (orphan)** — 7 years for the designated orphan indication. Blocks approval of
  the same drug for the same indication, not for other indications.
- **Paediatric exclusivity** — 6 months, added on top of patents *and* exclusivities in
  response to a written request. Frequently the binding constraint.
- **QIDP** — 5 additional years for qualified infectious disease products.

**Patent certifications in an ANDA:**

- Para I — no patent listed. Para II — patent expired. Para III — will wait for expiry.
- **Para IV** — patent invalid, unenforceable or not infringed. Filing triggers notice
  to the patentee; suing within 45 days imposes a **30-month stay** on approval.
- **§viii statement** — the generic carves out a patented indication with a skinny
  label, entering for the rest. This is why method-of-use patents deserve a discount.

**180-day first-applicant exclusivity** goes to the first Para IV filer(s), with
forfeiture provisions. It shapes the *shape* of erosion: a single first filer produces
a slower six-month erosion, after which a cliff arrives when the rest enter.

## Biologics (BPCIA, 2010)

- **12 years** of reference-product exclusivity from first licensure; biosimilar
  applications may not be *submitted* until year 4.
- **Interchangeability** is a separate designation and is what permits pharmacy-level
  substitution without prescriber involvement. Without it, erosion depends on payer
  and provider decisions and is much slower.
- The **"patent dance"** (§262(l)) governs information exchange and shapes litigation
  timing; participation is now effectively optional in practice, which has made
  timelines less predictable.
- The Purple Book lists licensure dates, exclusivity expiry and interchangeability.

## Erosion shapes to hold in mind

- **Oral solid, multiple ANDA filers:** 80–90% volume loss within 12 months; price to
  low single-digit percentages of brand within 24. Automatic substitution does the work.
- **Oral solid, single first filer for 180 days:** a slower step, then a cliff.
- **Injectable / infused biologic without interchangeability:** 15–40% over 2–3 years,
  price-led rather than volume-led, contested contract by contract.
- **Interchangeable biosimilar with several entrants:** materially faster, converging
  toward small-molecule behaviour but rarely reaching it.
- **Device-drug combination:** slowest; the delivery system is its own barrier.
